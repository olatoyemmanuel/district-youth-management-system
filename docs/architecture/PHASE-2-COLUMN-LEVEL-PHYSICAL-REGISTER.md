# DYMS Phase 2 Column-Level Physical Register

**Status:** Complete transcription of the 65-table typed column baseline; definition-level gaps remain open  
**Scope:** DYMS v1.0, Sango District  
**Implementation:** NOT AUTHORIZED  
**Source revision:** Current Phase 2 physical database design and FK relationship register

## 1. Purpose

This register expands the 65-table column inventory into one row per column. It consolidates:

- PostgreSQL type and nullability from the FK/column register, reconciled against the physical table definitions.
- Explicit default expressions, where the source defines one.
- Primary-key, unique-index and CHECK participation declared in the current design.
- FK constraint participation and per-relationship actions from the 155-row named FK register.
- Supporting index names from the 211-name canonical index inventory.
- Owner decision and approval status.

The register is a design transcription and gap register. It is not executable DDL and does not imply approval of the physical database design.

## 4. Outstanding column-level decisions

1. Defaults and generation. Most columns have no default expression stated in the source. UUID generation authority remains ODR-ENV-003. Timestamp authority and default policy remain ODR-ENV-005. Do not add defaults based on convention.
2. Constraint names. Primary-key and unique-index proposals are identified, but every CHECK constraint still needs a stable name, exact expression, and validation test before DDL.
3. State values. State-bearing columns remain subject to the lifecycle catalogue. PROPOSED and POLICY BLOCKED values are not approved CHECK-constraint enumerations or seed values.
4. Scope contracts. Exactly-one and zero-or-one scope rules require approved policy and complete cross-table consistency verification.
5. Partial unique indexes. Lifecycle-dependent predicates remain blocked until their state catalogues and transitions are approved.
6. Sensitive data and retention. Retention, audit, credential evidence and notification payload fields remain subject to their ODR policies and data-minimization rules.
7. Database compatibility. PostgreSQL target version, extensions, collation, UUID generation and production roles remain open under ODR-ENV-001–007.

## 5. DDL readiness
